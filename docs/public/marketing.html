Soft Architecture

Most agent frameworks rely on rigid control hierarchies or chaotic autonomy. Coleo occupies the evolutionary niche between: coordinated independence.

Decentralized Intelligence

Two-thirds of an octopus's neurons are in its arms, not its head. Coleo Arms possess their own memory, tools, and decision capacity - they develop local expertise while remaining accountable to the whole.

Governance Through Persuasion

The Brain does not command—it evaluates. Arms submit structured proposals with reasoning and wait for approval. This creates coordination without the rigidity of command chains or the chaos of pure autonomy.

Safe Experimentation

Each Arm operates within its own isolated Garden—an ephemeral environment where work happens without affecting the shared codebase until explicitly approved and integrated.

The Architecture

🧠

The Brain

The central coordination point. The Brain maintains architectural standards, evaluates incoming proposals from Arms, and manages the prioritization queue. It holds the long-term context that individual Arms cannot see.

🐙

Arms

Autonomous workers with specialized capabilities. Each Arm operates within its own context window and toolset, executing tasks and submitting structured proposals back to the Brain for approval.

🌿

Gardens

Isolated execution contexts. Gardens provide Arms with safe spaces to work - complete with file systems, tool access, and memory - without risking contamination of the main codebase or other Arms' work.

📡

The Observatory

The monitoring interface. Like the statocyst organ that helps cephalopods maintain orientation, the Observatory tracks activity across all Arms and Gardens, providing visibility into distributed operations.

How Coordination Works

1
Spawn

The Brain instantiates Arms with specific roles and assigns them to Gardens with relevant context and tools.

2
Execute

Arms work independently within their Gardens, accessing tools and modifying local state to accomplish tasks.

3
Propose

When ready, Arms submit structured proposals to the Brain—describing intended changes, reasoning, and impact.

4
Integrate

The Brain evaluates proposals against architectural constraints and either approves integration or requests revisions.
